# Focus

Step 4 of the flow in `SKILL.md`, from chapter 4 of *Storytelling with Data*, "focus your audience's attention". A few visual properties are seen before any reading happens: color, size, weight, position, an added mark, an enclosure. Used on one thing they send the eye there. Used on everything they send it nowhere.

## Bring the point forward

- Start with everything grey, then bring forward what the Big Idea is about and nothing else: one accent color, a heavier line, value labels on only the data the argument uses. Highlighting one thing makes the rest harder to see, which is what an explanation wants and what exploring the data doesn't.
- Highlight about a tenth of the page at most; past that nothing stands out.
- Size means importance. Things equally important are the same size, and the most important thing is the largest.
- The eye starts at the top left and works across and down. The conclusion goes there, and scales run the way people expect: negative on the left, positive on the right.
- Words can carry the emphasis too: in an annotation, set the phrase that states the point in the accent color of the data it explains, or in bold.

## Color

- Length and position are read as quantity. Hue isn't: nobody thinks orange is more than blue. Use a single color from light to dark when the color has to say "more".
- One accent color, used for the thing the title talks about and for the words that refer to it. A second accent only when two ends of a scale both matter, blue for the good end and orange for the bad one.
- A color keeps its meaning through the piece. A change of color tells the reader something changed.
- About one man in twelve can't tell red from green. Don't make that pair the difference between good and bad without a sign, a label, or a difference in brightness as well.
- Color sets tone. Muted greys with one blue read as calm; black and bold type read as formal. Match the tone in the brief.

## The look-away test

Look away, look back, and notice what you see first, second, and third. That order should be the order of the argument. If it isn't, change the emphasis until it is.
