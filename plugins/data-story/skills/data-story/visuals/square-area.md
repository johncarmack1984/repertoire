# Square areas

The chooser's row in `SKILL.md` for numbers so different in size that a bar would hide the small one. From chapter 2 of *Storytelling with Data*, where it is the one area graph she uses.

People judge areas badly, so area graphs are otherwise left out. The exception is a pair of numbers far apart in size, where the bar for the small one would be a sliver: squares drawn to scale show both.

- Make each square's area proportional to its value, so its side is proportional to the square root of the value. A value forty times as large gets a square about six and a third times as wide.
- Set the small square in a corner of the large one, or beside it on one baseline.
- Write each value and what it is next to its square, since nobody reads the ratio of two areas precisely.
- Say the ratio in words ("one in forty").

When the reader needs only the ratio, a sentence does it better: go back to the chooser.
