# A waterfall

The chooser's row in `SKILL.md` for where a value started, what was added and taken away, and where it ended. From chapter 2 of *Storytelling with Data*.

A waterfall pulls one total apart into the steps that took it from its start to its end: a budget through each change to the final figure, a backlog from the start of a quarter through what was opened and what was closed.

- The first and the last bar stand on zero and show the two totals.
- Each bar between them floats: it starts where the running total stood and runs up for an addition or down for a subtraction, so the reader can see the running total without reading a number.
- Order the steps the way the reader thinks about them: the additions and then the subtractions, or in the order they happened when the timing matters.
- Write each step's signed value on it (+12, −5) and each total's value on its bar. With every bar labelled, drop the y-axis.
- Two colors at most: one for the totals, and the accent for the step the Big Idea is about. Up and down already show in the direction and the sign, so they don't need red and green.
- A thin line from the top of one bar to the start of the next helps the eye across a wide chart; leave it out when the bars are close.
