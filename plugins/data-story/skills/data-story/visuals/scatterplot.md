# A scatterplot

The chooser's row in `SKILL.md` for how two measures relate across many items. From chapter 2 of *Storytelling with Data*.

A scatterplot puts one item at each point, one measure across and the other up, to show whether and how the two relate.

- Put the measure the reader thinks of as the cause, or can control, across, and the result up: request size across, response time up.
- Give both axes a title with its unit; position is all a point has, so the axes have to say what it means.
- Add a reference line (the average, a target, a break-even) and label it on the line, so above and below mean something.
- The accent goes on the points the title talks about (those above the line, the outliers, one group), and a note says in words what they have in common.
- Unlike bars, an axis may start above zero, since position is read and not length, but the labels must make the range plain.
- Draw a fitted line only when the relationship is the point and the fit is honest, and say what it is.

The title says what the relationship means for the reader: a cloud of points says nothing on its own.
