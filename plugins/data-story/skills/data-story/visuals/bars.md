# Bars

The chooser's row in `SKILL.md` for how categories compare. From chapter 2 of *Storytelling with Data*, with chapter 9's case on putting categories in a logical order.

Bars are for categories. The eye compares where the bars end, so the comparison is only true when they all start at zero.

- Bars start at zero, always. A bar chart whose axis starts at 90 makes 92 against 96 look like a threefold jump, and one reader who notices discards the argument along with the chart. When the differences that matter are small next to the totals, show the differences themselves, or put the numbers in a sentence.
- Horizontal bars are the default for categories. The names read left to right before the bar does, and long names fit without being rotated.
- Vertical bars suit a few categories with short names, or periods of time.
- With two series, what is adjacent gets compared, so group the bars by the comparison that matters.
- Make the bars wider than the gaps between them.
- Sorted by size, the end the reader should look at goes on top.
- A value written on a bar goes at its end, just past the bar or just inside it.
