# A sentence

The chooser's first row in `SKILL.md`: one or two numbers, or one before and after. Chapter 2 of *Storytelling with Data* opens its visuals with simple text, for when there are only a number or two to share.

One or two numbers go in a sentence that has the numbers in it: "Median build time fell from 14 minutes to 9." One before and after is still two numbers, even when each comes with a range or a run count: those go in the same sentence or the next one. A chart or a table around two numbers costs space and makes them look smaller, so the sentence stands alone. Shown alone on a slide, the number is set large, with a few words under it saying what it is.

Be careful when collapsing several numbers into one. "Halved" hides whether it went from 40 to 20 or from 4 to 2, so keep both values when their size matters to the reader.

## Steps 3 to 5 for a sentence

- Nothing repeats it: no table, no chart, and no bullet list of the same values beside it.
- It comes first, where the eye starts. In markdown, a number that matters can sit on a line of its own, or be the one bold phrase.
- It is the Big Idea with its numbers in it, and the next sentence says what they are: for measured runs, the statistic, the run count, and the spread, as `reference/measurements.md` describes.
