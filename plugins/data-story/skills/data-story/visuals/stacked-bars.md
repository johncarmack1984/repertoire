# Stacked bars

The chooser's row in `SKILL.md` for a total and its parts, parts of a whole across a few groups, or answers on a scale. From chapter 2 of *Storytelling with Data*, with chapter 9's case on alternatives to pies.

## A total and its parts

Stacked bars show each total and what it is made of. Only the bottom segment sits on the shared zero baseline; every segment above it floats, so its size is hard to compare from bar to bar. Use them when the total and one part are the point, put that part at the bottom, and give it the accent color with the rest in greys.

## Parts of a whole: 100% stacked bars

Each bar is one group scaled to 100%, so the reader compares shares rather than sizes. The first and last segments both sit on a baseline, at the left and right ends, so those two are easy to compare across groups.

- Draw them horizontally, one bar per group, stacked one above another.
- Name the segments once, over the top bar, and write the percentages in the segments where they fit.
- The bars hide how many each group holds, so give the group sizes in a note.

## Answers on a scale

For answers on a scale (strongly disagree to strongly agree), run the segments in the scale's order, negative on the left. Give the two ends their colors, blue for the good end and orange for the bad, and leave the neutral middle grey. When one side is the point, color only that side.
