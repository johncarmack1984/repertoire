# A slopegraph

The chooser's row in `SKILL.md` for what changed between two points in time, or two conditions, across several categories. From chapter 2 of *Storytelling with Data*.

A slopegraph draws each category as one line from its first value on the left to its second on the right. It shows the values, the direction, and the size of each change together, and one line can be emphasized against the rest.

- Two columns of points, one per time or condition, each named once at the top or the bottom ("Q1", "Q3"; "before", "after").
- Both columns share one vertical scale, so a steeper line is a bigger change.
- Label every line at both ends: the category's name and its first value on the left, its second value on the right. The labels replace the y-axis, so draw none.
- The accent goes on the line the Big Idea is about, and on its labels at both ends.
- When two labels collide because their values are close, nudge the labels apart and leave the points where they are.

It stops working when many lines pile up in a narrow band, and when the reader needs the categories in an order of your choosing, since the values set the order. Then go back to the chooser for bars: two bars per category, grouped by category, with the change written beside each pair.
