# Brief

Working notes for this map, never part of it; step 2 of `SKILL.md` says what each line holds, and every line is filled before anything is drawn.

```text
Reader: readers of a blog post on 2024's state-to-state moves, general audience, reading on desktop or phone; they decide nothing operational, they come away knowing where people who left their state went.
Task: find the largest flows (and read their direction).
Question: Where did Americans go when they moved to another state in 2024? The title answers it: "Of the 50 biggest moves between states in 2024, 40 went next door or to Florida or Texas" (26 of the 50 cross one state border, and 14 of the other 24 end in Florida or Texas; the critique replaced "Most of 2024's biggest moves..." with the count and the cut, because the cold reader dropped both the "next door" half and the 23% limit).
Data: OD pairs between 52 places (50 states, DC, Puerto Rico), nodes at the 2020 Census centers of population; count; people aged 1 and over who lived in another state one year earlier; no denominator on the map (the 7,188,344 published movers are the base for the share kept); 2,373 published pairs, 2,097 of them nonzero and 276 zero estimates (inspect counts both), plus 279 pairs the Census suppressed as "N" (too few sample cases); 2024 (residence one year before the ACS interview); U.S. Census Bureau, 2024 ACS 1-year State-to-State Migration Flows, Table 1, with a 90% margin of error on every pair (inspect pairs the moe column with movers: CV 1% to 128%, median 39%, and neighboring ranks tie from rank 2 to 10, P36); boundaries from the Census 2023 cartographic boundary file at 1:20m.
Supports: which flows are among the largest; that California to Texas (77,161 ±9,059) is the largest single flow, larger than the next (New York to New Jersey, 56,799 ±8,244) by more than the margin of error of the difference; the direction of a pair whose two flows differ by more than that margin (New York to New Jersey 56,799 vs back 36,002); that of the 50 largest flows, 26 cross one state border and 14 of the other 24 end in Florida or Texas. It can't support: exact ranks among flows within each other's margin (ranks 2 to 7, 50,661 to 56,799, are tied); a sharp line at the 50th flow (21 kept flows and 36 cut ones are within the margin of the 50th); net gains or losses from one line; that most movers go next door (only 30% of all 7.19 million moved to a bordering state); rates per resident (big flows join big states); why people moved; moves within a state or to and from abroad.
Location: the question is where people went, and the answer is geographic: short hops across one border, and long moves that converge on Florida and Texas. A list can rank the flows but can't show which cross one border and which cross the country.
Candidates: (1) curved flow map of the largest flows; its default lie: lines drawn straight or on top of each other hide direction and bury each other, long flows get more ink than short ones of the same size, and the cut hides most movers while looking like the whole picture. (2) choropleth of net migration per 1,000 residents; its default lie: it shows who gained and lost, not where anyone went, and a net hides two big gross flows (California to Texas 77,161 and back 45,447 become one 31,714). (3) sorted bars of the largest flows, no map; its default lie: it ranks flows the margins of error tie, and it hides the geography that answers the question.
Idiom: flows row, curved flows on an equal-area map of the regional extent (OD pairs, task is find the largest flows and their direction); the row above (isarithmic) is for a continuous field, which this is not, and the cheaper no-map row loses the geography the question asks for, so a sorted list goes beside the map instead. Guardrails against flows.md's default lies: the kept-and-omitted line from flows --top in the subtitle and legend, with the share of movers it keeps (P18); the same cut keeps the map to 50 flows, under the 100 the lever warns at; --geometry curved bends each flow to the right of travel, so the 17 reciprocal pairs among the 50 separate (P15); the lever clears curves around the 26 drawn nodes and flips or bends 9 of them (P16); arrowheads scale with width and grow on thin flows (P17); small flows draw on top (P19); no kept flow touches Alaska, Hawaii, or Puerto Rico, so nothing wraps (P20); flat lines, no raised arcs (P22); and the fade by magnitude keeps the long thin arcs from out-inking the short heavy ones. From the data: margins of error tie neighboring ranks, so the companion prints each estimate with its margin and the text claims no exact rank but the first.
Companions: a sorted table of the ten largest moves beside the map, with each estimate and its 90% margin of error (headed "Give or take"), for readers who want exact values; its heading says "after the top one, the order is uncertain" (every adjacent pair from rank 2 to 11 differs by less than the margin of the difference; rank 1 beats rank 2 by 20,362 against a margin of 12,249).
Visual plan: the flow lines dominate, in the lever's one warm hue, #a63603, a dark orange-brown with no alarm red in it (flows.md, Slocum §20.4), faded by value from 0.45 at the smallest kept flow (21,660) to 1 at the largest (77,161) so ink follows movers, not distance; width linear in movers with --max-width 8 (the lever's 12 drew 30 px heads that covered neighboring flows and names), the 1 px floor never reached (the thinnest kept flow is 2.25 px); arrowheads 2.5 times the width, at least 6 px; the lever's node circles (gross movers in and out across all 2,097 flows, area-true, dark gray) mark where flows meet; the library puts each state's name in the corner its flows, arrowheads, and neighbors leave clearest, so all 26 render and no head covers one; state fills recede in pale gray with white borders and no basemap tiles. Left out: every flow below the cut, said in the subtitle and legend with the share kept; Alaska, Hawaii, and Puerto Rico, since none is in a kept flow (their largest are Puerto Rico to Florida, 9,395, rank 187; California to Hawaii, 10,516, rank 161; Alaska to Colorado, 3,700, rank 489), said in the credits line; roads, cities, and relief. Tone: plain, editorial, one point.
Interface: static PNG for a blog, designed at 1200 x 654 CSS px and captured at device scale 2 (dm-spec figure, so check renders it there); read on desktop, or on a phone where the PNG shrinks; 10 to 30 seconds; guided; refused: zoom and pan (dm-spec zoom held at the fitted view), rotate, popups, animation, navigation and attribution controls, basemap tiles. The library's hover tooltips stay in the HTML page but never reach the PNG.
Projection: Albers equal-area conic, custom:albers, +proj=aea +lat_1=28.65 +lat_2=45.25 +lat_0=36.95 +lon_0=-95.95 +x_0=0 +y_0=0 +datum=WGS84 +units=m +no_defs, as datamap.py crs prints it for the lower 48's extent (-125, 24.5, -66.9, 49.4); the conic rule in projection.md (parallels one-sixth in from each limit) for a mid-latitude extent wider east to west. Flows don't need equal area, but the stack row for a static figure registers an equal-area CRS and readers know the US in Albers. The view fits the flows and nodes, which shows all of the lower 48; the extent's [W, S, E, N] box is a wide fan in Albers that draws the map about a tenth smaller.
Stack: maplibre with a registered equal-area CRS, captured at device scale 2, the stack row for a static figure.
Sensitive: none. Published state-level estimates; the Census already suppressed the small cells ("N"), and no zero or suppressed pair is near the cut.
Answer key: (1) Which single move between two states was the largest in 2024? California to Texas, 77,161 movers (±9,059). (2) Which state is the destination of the most of the flows shown? Florida, 10 of the 50 (Texas is next with 6). (3) Between New York and New Jersey, which direction carries more movers? New York to New Jersey, 56,799 vs 36,002 back (the gap of 20,797 exceeds the ±10,053 margin of the difference).
Departures: (1) P16 stays open where three flows cross four circles they don't connect, which no bend within the lever's caps clears: Florida to Pennsylvania over Maryland's by 6.3 px, Florida to New York over Virginia's and Maryland's by 3.6 and 2.6 px, and New York to California over Nevada's by 1 px. (2) flows ran at the lever's default --view, 844 x 634, not the figure's map, 844 x 493, against flows.md's advice to pass the real size. Both fit the map by its width at zoom 3.45, so every gap the lever reports holds on the page, and the default's deeper bend cap, 38 px against 30, clears two more flows and leaves Maryland's and Virginia's names each beside its own circle, where at 30 px Virginia's name sat level with Maryland's circle. (3) The 11 px labels fall under design.md's 9 px floor wherever the 1200 px figure shows narrower than about 980 px, as on a phone.
```
